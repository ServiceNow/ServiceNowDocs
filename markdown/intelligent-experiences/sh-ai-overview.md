# Shadow AI overview

Identify unsanctioned AI use across your organization by reviewing detected traffic, AI-generated recommendations, detection methods, and policy impact.

## Key benefits

-   See your organization's AI exposure at a glance, without opening individual service records.
-   Spot risk patterns automatically, such as a single user driving nearly all use of a service or a sudden week-over-week spike, instead of searching through a list.
-   View detected AI use by service, by user, or by department.
-   Keep detection focused on what your organization actually cares about by curating the registry, rather than reviewing traffic from domains that aren't a concern.

## Required roles

The AI steward \[sn\_ai\_governance.ai\_steward\] role is required to view the Shadow AI overview.

## Accessing the Shadow AI overview

View the Shadow AI overview by navigating to **All** &gt; **AI Control Tower** &gt; **Home** &gt; **Inventory** &gt; **Shadow AI**.

## Top action items

AI Control Tower surfaces AI-generated recommendations for risk patterns that require your attention, such as a single user driving most of a service's use or adoption spreading broadly across departments.

**Important:** Top action items are generated by automated analysis of detected traffic patterns. Review the underlying traffic before acting on a recommendation.

The same list, scoped to just that service, also appears on an individual service's own record. Selecting the action on a recommendation opens the detected service's record.

## Detected AI traffic

Review the services, users, and departments found by the Shadow AI detection methods in the **Detected AI traffic** list.

Narrow usage to a specific time period by customizing the date range in the date picker. Select any row to open the service, user, or department record for more detail. See [Review AI traffic detected across your organization](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ServiceNow/ServiceNowDocs/zurich/markdown/zurich/intelligent-experiences/sh-ai-review-detected-traffic.md).

-   **__AI traffic__**

    Lists each detected service, so you can see which services are in use before checking who's using them.

    -   View the detection methods that reported the service in the **Detection** column. Each detection method provides a different level of detail related to the service. For example, you can view the content of a request sent to an AI service that was detected by Agent Client Collector \(ACC\).
    -   Determine whether a policy is applied to manage service availability and whether it's completely blocked from use or partially available to some users by checking the **Policy** column. A service without an applied policy poses the highest risk.
    -   View the number of individual requests made to the detected service within the selected date range in the **Events** column.
    -   View the total number of distinct users who initiated the events in the **Users** column. A high event count against a small number of users indicates concentrated use.
    -   Determine whether traffic was detected recently by checking the **Status** column. Set the date range to **All time** for a comprehensive overview.
        -   An AI service with reported traffic in the last 30 days is marked as New.
        -   An AI service with no traffic for 30 days is marked as Limited details, and only aggregate counts remain available for it.
        -   Additional status values appear based on specific triage actions taken by the AI steward.
-   **__Users__**

    Lists activity matched to a known user, grouped by department. Expand a department to see each user in the department that used the detected service. Determine which people are using a detected AI service the most and from what device. Check the number of individual requests made to the detected service by a specific user in the **Events** column.

-   **__Departments__**

    Lists each department. Use this subtab to find where adoption is concentrated, which is often the fastest route to the team that can explain why a service is in use.


## Key details and detection methods

Check coverage distribution by detection method in the selected time period in the **Detection methods** panel. For each method, see how many detected AI services it reported, the count as a share of the total, and how long ago it last synced.

Because more than one method can report the same service, counts can overlap and add up to more than the total number of detected services. A method with low coverage isn't a fault, it reflects where that method can observe. ACC reports from the devices where its agent is deployed, and Armis reports across the network. Connecting more than one gives the widest coverage.

Confirm data is still flowing by checking the last sync time. ACC reports each request as it happens, so its sync time stays recent. Armis reports hourly, so a sync time up to an hour old is typical.

## Tracking AI services in the registry

In the registry, determine which AI services require detection rather than enforcing it on every domain. The registry list reflects what your organization actually wants tracked and ignores services that don't require tracking. For example, AI traffic is only detected for AI services that you mark as active. To stop sending traffic for a specific AI service, you can mark it as Not Shadow AI. For details, see [Manage the AI services registry](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ServiceNow/ServiceNowDocs/zurich/markdown/zurich/intelligent-experiences/sh-ai-manage-registry.md).

## When no detected traffic appears

An empty **Detected AI traffic** list after setup usually means one of the following, in the order worth checking.

-   No detection source is connected and active. Detection begins only after at least one source is connected. Check the **Detection methods** panel for a recent sync time. For more information, check the connection state and processing state by navigating to **Settings** &gt; **Integrations** &gt; **Connectors** &gt; **Established connections**.
-   Not enough time has passed. ACC reports each request as it happens, so its detections appear soon after activity occurs. Armis reports hourly at the device and host level, so allow at least an hour before expecting network detections.
-   The date range excludes the activity. A narrow range hides services whose last activity falls outside it. Set the range to **All time** to see everything detected so far.
-   A filter is still applied. The **Status** filter in particular hides snoozed and dismissed services.
-   The registry doesn't recognize the domain. A service your organization uses appears only when the registry treats its domain as an AI service. See [Manage the AI services registry](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ServiceNow/ServiceNowDocs/zurich/markdown/zurich/intelligent-experiences/sh-ai-manage-registry.md).

## Use cases

-   **Running a weekly exposure review**

    An AI steward opens the Shadow AI overview on Monday morning and sets the date range to the last 7 days. Two services appear that weren't there the previous week, both matched to the same provider the organization already has an agreement with. The AI steward reviews the top action items, finds no critical patterns, and opens each new service to check its user count and volume. The AI steward determines that a single user is testing one service and uses the snooze action while pending a conversation with that person's manager. The other shows use across three departments, so the AI steward keeps it under review and raises it with the team that owns the organization's approved AI tools.

-   **Investigating a sudden increase in use**

    A recommendation reports a week-over-week usage spike for a transcription service. The AI steward opens the **Departments** subtab and sees the increase is confined to one team. Opening the service and reviewing the **Events** view shows the traffic starting on a specific date, which the AI steward recognizes as the week that team began a customer research project. The use is legitimate, so rather than blocking it, the AI steward records the finding and raises the gap with the team that owns the organization's approved AI tools.

-   **Preparing for a governance or audit review**

    An AI steward needs to show what unsanctioned AI use exists and what was done about each finding. Setting the date range to **All time** gives the full picture, including services now in **Limited details** status. The **Policy** column separates services with a block policy from those without one. For any individual finding, the **Activity** section on the service's record shows each change, who or what made it, and when, which is the evidence trail the review needs.
