# Managing change for AI adoption

A structured organizational change management approach helps users understand, embrace, and effectively leverage AI capabilities in their daily work.

The difference between successful AI adoption and underutilized capabilities often comes down to how well you manage the human side of change. ServiceNow AI requires more active change management than traditional products because end users can choose whether to use AI-powered skills. A structured organizational change management \(OCM\) approach helps users understand, embrace, and effectively leverage AI capabilities in their daily work.

## Why change management matters for AI adoption

Projects with strong change management are significantly more successful than those without structured adoption programs. With generative AI, the stakes are even higher:

-   User choice drives adoption. Unlike mandatory system upgrades, AI features are optional tools that users must actively choose to incorporate into their workflows.
-   AI changes how people work. Generative AI introduces new interaction patterns that may feel unfamiliar, requiring users to trust AI-generated outputs and adapt established habits.
-   Concerns must be addressed proactively. Users may have questions about trust, accuracy, job impact, and data security that require thoughtful communication.
-   Value depends on usage. The return on investment of is directly tied to adoption rates. Features that go unused deliver no business value regardless of their technical capabilities.

## Four pillars of effective change management

Successful AI adoption programs address four interconnected areas: stakeholders and champions, messaging and communication, training and enablement, and reinforcement and optimization.

-   **Identify stakeholders and champions**

    Build a network of people who drive adoption across the organization:

    -   Executive sponsors provide visible leadership support and communicate strategic importance.
    -   Business partners take responsibility for messaging within their teams and hold accountability for adoption outcomes.
    -   Change champions are influential frontline employees or mid-level managers who advocate for AI adoption, help train colleagues, and provide valuable feedback.
    -   Subject matter experts confirm that AI capabilities align with actual workflow needs and validate that outputs meet quality standards.
    Engage business partners early and assign clear ownership. Collaboration between IT and business teams is critical, with business partners taking responsibility for encouraging their teams to drive adoption. Communication is most effective when partners use language that emphasizes personal benefits to individual users.

-   **Craft clear messaging**

    Develop communications that help users understand why AI matters and how it improves their work:

    -   Lead with benefits. Frame AI capabilities in terms of the problems they solve and the time they save, not just the features they provide.
    -   Address concerns directly. Proactively communicate about trust, accuracy, job impact, and data security through strategic messaging rather than waiting for questions to arise.
    -   Use multiple channels. Communicate through champions, focus groups, and interactive sessions rather than relying solely on email announcements.
    -   Share success stories. Highlight teams that have seen tangible improvements, such as reduced case review time or faster resolution rates, to build momentum and encourage broader adoption.
    Meet users where they are by adapting communication approaches to your organization's preferences, whether virtual, in-office, or remote-first environments.

-   **Provide effective training**

    Confirm that users are prepared to use AI successfully through structured enablement:

    -   Develop role-based training. Different user personas interact with AI in different ways. Tailor training content to specific roles and use cases.
    -   Use hands-on approaches. Live demos, office hours, and interactive sessions outperform static documentation or slide presentations.
    -   Make training ongoing. Enablement is not a one-time event. Provide continuous learning opportunities as capabilities evolve and users gain experience.
    -   Provide digital enablement. Multimedia content, onboarding resources, and self-service learning materials extend the reach of your training program.
-   **Reinforce and optimize**

    Track progress, gather feedback, and make continuous improvements:

    -   Measure adoption metrics. Track monthly active user percentages, feature usage rates, and user sentiment to understand adoption patterns.
    -   Collect and act on feedback. Establish channels for users to report issues, suggest improvements, and share experiences.
    -   Celebrate wins. Recognize teams and individuals who achieve measurable improvements through AI adoption.
    -   Iterate based on data. Use adoption analytics to identify areas for improvement and adjust your approach accordingly.

## Recommended rollout strategies

How you introduce AI capabilities significantly impacts adoption success. Consider these proven approaches:

-   **Start with pilots**

    Groups involved in pilots consistently show the highest adoption rates. Pilot programs allow you to gather feedback, refine training approaches, and demonstrate success before broader rollout. Study your users, understand their roles, and target the rollout with assigned access and permissions for active users. Without this focus, non-engaged users can skew adoption metrics.

-   **Use phased rollouts**

    Implement iteratively with quick feedback loops rather than large, one-time rollouts. Phased approaches combined with persuasion-based communication help move users from curiosity to confident adoption. This approach also allows you to incorporate lessons learned from earlier phases into subsequent ones.

-   **Start with quick wins**

    Begin with turnkey capabilities such as incident or case summarization that deliver immediate, visible value. Quick wins help you gain insights into user experience, value measurement, and change management while building foundational readiness for more advanced features.

-   **Prioritize user experience**

    Features that require users to change their behavior need strong user experience design. Early UX research and limited rollouts help optimize the experience before broad deployment. Even small details matter: if a summarize button is positioned outside users' natural line of sight, adoption suffers regardless of how valuable the feature is.


## Measuring adoption success

Before launching any use case, identify the metrics you will use to gauge adoption and usage. Track these key indicators:

-   Adoption: the monthly active user percentage of AI users against total potential users.
-   Usage: which features are used and how often, based on triggers such as summaries successfully generated.
-   Sentiment: the proportion of positive feedback out of total feedback.
-   Accuracy: how well AI outputs meet quality standards for the use case, such as search or conversation success rates and summary or code acceptance rates.
-   Hours saved: the time savings that form the basis of value calculations for both fulfiller and requester use cases.

Track adoption metrics, analyze them for trends, and layer qualitative and quantitative data into the story to demonstrate impact and identify opportunities for improvement.

## Driving ongoing engagement

Sustained adoption requires ongoing attention. Consider these approaches to maintain momentum:

-   Promote a culture shift. Position generative AI as a trusted partner that enhances human capabilities rather than replacing them.
-   Introduce gamification. Programs that reward high-quality usage, such as recognizing agents who generate the best knowledge base articles using AI, can increase engagement.
-   Share success broadly. Feature success stories in newsletters, all-hands meetings, and internal communications to inspire others.
-   Respond to feedback quickly. Demonstrate that user input drives improvements by acting on feedback and communicating changes.
